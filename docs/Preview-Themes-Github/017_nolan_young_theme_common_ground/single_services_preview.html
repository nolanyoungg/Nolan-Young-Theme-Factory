All services / Renovations & additions

Good renovation begins with understanding what deserves to stay. We look for the smallest set of changes that can make a home work better: a clearer plan, a stronger garden connection, a little more daylight in the right place.

From reworking a single floor to a measured addition, the process brings your daily needs, the building’s condition, and the available budget into the same conversation.

The scope / What we consider

See the whole.
Resolve the details.

Layout & everyday use

Map how you move through the home, where belongings gather, and which rooms need privacy or connection. Compare options before adding area.

Light & comfort

Consider orientation, shading, ventilation opportunities, and the depth of the plan. Coordinate specialist input where performance needs closer investigation.

Character & materials

Identify elements worth retaining and assess how old and new can meet. Develop finishes and joinery with maintenance and repair in mind.

Buildability & sequence

Review structural changes, service routes, site access, and possible phases with the relevant consultants and builder. Make the work understandable before it begins.

A project proposal would define the exact appointment. Surveys, engineering, statutory fees, and construction costs are separate items to discuss.

The process

One clear decision at a time.

  1. 01

    Understand the existing

    Gather your brief, available drawings, and a condition overview. Identify the measured survey and specialist investigations needed to make informed decisions.

  2. 02

    Test the possibilities

    Explore a small number of layout options, including what could be achieved within the current footprint. Discuss priorities and seek cost input early.

  3. 03

    Develop the design

    Resolve the chosen arrangement, material direction, and key details. Coordinate consultant input and identify the relevant approval route for the site.

  4. 04

    Prepare for building

    Produce the agreed drawings and specifications for pricing and construction. Confirm responsibilities, information needs, and the scope of any site-stage support.

An example / Fictional concept

Courtyard House

A narrow house becomes easier to live in by moving circulation toward a small courtyard. The front rooms remain intact, while the shared kitchen and dining space gain a direct relationship with the garden.

The lesson is simple: a change in the plan can matter more than a change in size.

Renovation questions

A little clarity
before you start.

Can we live at home during the work?

Sometimes, but it depends on access, services, dust, noise, and the extent of structural work. Discuss this with the design team and builder early. A phased plan may help, while some projects need a temporary move.

Will we need planning permission?

That depends on the location, property, and proposed changes. The relevant local requirements would need to be checked for your actual project. This fictional sample cannot determine an approval route.

What if our budget is still uncertain?

Start with a comfortable investment range and a list of priorities. Early options can then be tested with appropriate cost advice. Keep professional fees, approvals, contingency, and temporary accommodation in the overall picture.

Can you guarantee a completion date?

No date can be established from an initial idea alone. Surveys, approvals, design decisions, contractor availability, and the condition of the existing building all affect the schedule. A realistic program would be developed as those unknowns become clearer.
